The fee story: $27,162 → $1,916.75

The City's July 23 estimate priced the full request at $27,162 — 1,086.5 hours of cell-by-cell review, mostly to redact the transfer-reason field for FERPA. After the appeal (SPR26/2861) and a request narrowing to the last three school years at district-wide aggregate level, the City's July 31 supplemental response cut the estimate to $1,916.75 — a 93% reduction. The Supervisor of Records also denied the City's request to charge for segregation/redaction (SPR26/2850, untimely petition). The fee petition and both determinations are linked above.

SECURITY

Worcester Public Schools phishing incident

On July 30, 2026, Parent Data Force notified Worcester Public Schools of an authenticated phishing email sent through the district's domain, impersonating Dr. Tammy Murray and directing recipients to a Netlify-hosted landing page (murraytammy.netlify.app). The analysis reports below were submitted to the district and are published here for public accountability.

Forensic Email Analysis Report

What it found: the message authenticated as Worcester mail — Gmail recorded it passing SPF/DKIM/DMARC checks — meaning ordinary anti-spoofing controls did not reject it. The message blind-copied a parent address whose source is unknown, and the sending mechanism (active account session, OAuth access, or compromised relay) must be identified by the district. The EML does not establish whole-domain compromise, but it does establish at least one district identity was used to send mail that Gmail treated as legitimate.

Why it matters: if the sending mechanism involved an active account session or broad OAuth access, the risk may extend beyond sending mail to reading messages, reviewing files, and accessing confidential special-education records.

Download the forensic report (.docx)

Privacy note: the public copy of this report is redacted to remove a recipient's personal name and email address. The unredacted version was provided to the district and the Supervisor of Records as appropriate.

Phishing Landing Page & Public-Risk Analysis

What it found: the email lured families with a "secure message" pretext to a Netlify-hosted page. The hostname currently does not resolve — consistent with removal, suspension, or expiration — so the exact landing-page fields and data-storage destination cannot be reconstructed from public sources. The deception analysis found the claimed identity, subject, secure-message language, signature, and destination domain all consistent with a credential-harvesting campaign.

Why it matters: families regularly receive confidential special-education records, evaluation materials, and meeting notices from the district — making a spoofed district message a particularly dangerous lure.
